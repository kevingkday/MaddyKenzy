What this is

A game that keeps rewriting itself, with a parent holding the pen.

Most kids' apps are designed once, for the average child. These games start out that way too, then adapt to the specific kids playing them: what they tap first, where they lose interest, which sounds they try, which letters they keep missing.

They're made for young children, including early talkers and kids who drift after a couple of taps. The first job is to grab attention. Learning (letters, sounds, counting, adding one, saying words) is built on top of that.

How it learns

Two loops: it improves the game, and it improves how it improves.

The inner loop changes the game. The outer loop measures those changes and rewrites the rules the AI follows when making them. A parent approves every step.

Inner loop: the game improves

  1. Kids play.

    Taps, answers, sounds, pops, time per activity, signs of drifting attention, all tagged to the child who's playing.

  2. Nightly review.

    Claude reads the new play and the family's goals and writes a suggestion: plain notes per child plus a changed game setup, one or two changes at a time.

  3. Safety tests.

    The suggestion is loaded into the real game and played automatically on iPad-sized screens. If anything breaks, it's thrown away.

  4. Parent applies.

    Nothing changes until a parent taps Apply. Undo is always one tap away.

Outer loop: the improver improves

A plain calculation, with no AI involved, compares how the kids played on each applied version against the one before: minutes per session, first-try accuracy, sounds made and signs of lost interest. Each change gets a verdict: helped, hurt, mixed or no change.

Once enough results are in, Claude reads that scorecard against its own earlier reasoning and rewrites its playbook, the rules it follows when it suggests changes. It keeps what worked and drops what didn't. The parent approves the new playbook too.

Example scorecard entry

v4 vs v3: ✅ Helped. Play per session 3.2 → 4.4 min (+38%); first-try right 61% → 64%; lost-interest signals 2.1 → 1.2.

Interactive voice

It talks in a parent's voice, and it listens back.

Talks

Every line Itsy says, about 450 of them (questions, letter sounds, counting, praise, "Can you say moo?"), plays in a parent's own cloned voice, made once with ElevenLabs.

If a line has no recording yet, the device voice says it, and it's recorded automatically that night.

Listens

In Say It!, Itsy shows a picture and asks the child to say it. Any sound counts: a babble or a try makes Itsy jump and sparkle. It rewards the attempt, not the perfect word.

The microphone only measures loudness on the device. Nothing is recorded or sent.

No app store

Installs from the browser. No approval, no waiting.

Each game is a progressive web app (PWA): a website that installs like an app. It opens full-screen from its own home-screen icon and works offline. An improvement made tonight is on the iPad tomorrow, without an App Store or Play Store review.

  • Instant updatesEvery approved change arrives on the next launch.

Privacy & safety

Public code. Private kids.

What's protected

  • Names, notes, goals and the AI's notes about each child are encrypted, with a separate key for every family, kept outside the database.
  • Private mode: a family can lock everything with a passphrase only they know. Not even the site owner can read it.
  • Families can download all their data or delete it permanently at any time.
  • The code is open source. The kids' data never goes into it.

What keeps it kid-safe

  • A parent approves every change. The AI only ever suggests.
  • Changes stay within set limits and must pass automated play tests on iPad screens.
  • Leaving a game needs a press-and-hold, so a stray tap does nothing.
  • An optional play timer ends with a goodbye song.
  • No ads, no purchases, no links out, no chat.
